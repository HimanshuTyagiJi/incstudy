Q1. Differentiate between Centralisation and Decentralisation.

🔥 Repeated: 2016 3 Marks
✍️ Model Answer:

Centralisation refers to the concentration of decision-making authority systematically at the top management level. Subordinates primarily execute decisions without independent discretion.

Decentralisation is the systematic, wide dispersal of decision-making authority down to middle and lower levels throughout the organization.

Q2. What are the barriers to effective communication in an organisation?

🔥 Repeated: 2016 3 Marks
✍️ Model Answer:

Communication barriers distort, delay, or block the transmission of ideas between sender and receiver. The four primary classes are:

  1. Physical / Environmental Barriers: Distance, workplace noise, faulty telecom equipment, and information overload.
  2. Semantic / Language Barriers: Jargon, poorly translated messages, and ambiguous words with multiple meanings.
  3. Psychological / Emotional Barriers: Premature evaluation, distrust, fear of authority, anger, and defensive attitudes.
  4. Organisational Barriers: Rigid scalar chain, excessive hierarchical layers, restrictive policies, and lack of grievance channels.

Q3. How does environment affect organisational change?

🔥 Repeated: 2016 3 Marks
✍️ Model Answer:

Organizations operate as open dynamic systems that continuously interact with their environment. Environmental forces trigger mandatory internal adaptations:

  • External Environment Forces:
    • Technological Shifts: Emergence of AI, automation, or cloud forces companies to overhaul legacy IT infrastructure.
    • Competitive Pressures: New market entrants and price wars compel strategic pivots.
    • Regulatory & Legal: Changes in tax laws (e.g., GST), data privacy (GDPR), or labor safety standards.
    • Socio-Cultural: Shifting consumer preferences and expectations of remote/hybrid work.
  • Internal Environment Forces: Leadership changes, union demands, workforce diversity, and declining profitability.

Q4. Explain any two business strategies.

🔥 Repeated: 2016 3 Marks
✍️ Model Answer:

According to Michael Porter's Generic Strategies Framework, businesses achieve sustainable competitive advantage primarily through:

  1. Cost Leadership Strategy: The enterprise aims to become the lowest-cost producer in its industry through economies of scale, strict cost control, lean supply chains, and standardized processes (e.g., Walmart, DMart, Southwest Airlines).
  2. Differentiation Strategy: The enterprise designs unique, premium products or services distinguished by superior quality, brand prestige, advanced technology, or exceptional customer care, enabling premium pricing (e.g., Apple, Rolex, Tesla).

Q5. What are the various leadership styles?

🔥 Repeated: 2016, 2020 3 Marks
✍️ Model Answer:

Leadership style defines the behavioral pattern a leader uses to guide, motivate, and direct teams:

  • 1. Autocratic (Authoritarian): Centralizes complete decision power. Employs one-way communication; demands strict adherence without subordinate input. Best for emergency crises.
  • 2. Democratic (Participative): Encourages team consultation, consensus, and joint decision-making. Boosts employee satisfaction and creative problem-solving.
  • 3. Laissez-Faire (Free-Rein): Grants complete autonomy and decision freedom to competent, highly self-motivated specialists; leader acts purely as an advisor/facilitator.
  • 4. Transformational: Inspires and motivates followers through a compelling future vision, charisma, intellectual stimulation, and cultural innovation.

Q6. What are the features of Total Quality Management (TQM)?

🔥 Repeated: 2017 3 Marks
✍️ Model Answer:

Total Quality Management (TQM) is an organization-wide management approach focused on continuous quality improvement in all products, services, and internal processes:

  1. Customer Focus: Quality is defined by customer satisfaction and meeting or exceeding their expectations.
  2. Continuous Improvement (Kaizen): Unending drive to eliminate defects, minimize rework, and improve process efficiency.
  3. Total Employee Involvement: Empowering workers across all levels through cross-functional quality circles.
  4. Process-Centric Thinking: Focus on refining workflows rather than merely inspecting end outputs.
  5. Fact-Based Decision Making: Relying on statistical process control (SPC) and measurable data metrics.

Q7. What are the essential qualities of an effective leader?

🔥 Repeated: 2017 3 Marks
✍️ Model Answer:

An effective leader combines innate personality traits with learned behavioral proficiencies:

  • Strategic Vision: Ability to foresee future market trends and articulate an inspiring organizational direction.
  • High Emotional Intelligence (EQ): Empathy, self-regulation, and strong interpersonal sensitivity to build high-trust teams.
  • Integrity & Moral Character: Honesty, transparency, and ethical consistency that earns loyalty and respect.
  • Decisiveness: Courage to take timely, calculated decisions under conditions of risk and ambiguity.
  • Empowerment & Coaching: Nurturing talent, delegating authority, and celebrating team accomplishments.

Q8. State the features and nature of motivation.

🔥 Repeated: 2017, 2020 3 Marks
✍️ Model Answer:

Motivation is the internal driving psychological force that stimulates, channels, and sustains goal-directed human behavior:

  1. Psychological Phenomenon: It originates internally within an individual's subconscious needs and desires.
  2. Goal-Oriented: Motivation channels human energy toward achieving specific targets (e.g., career promotion, bonus).
  3. Continuous Dynamic Process: Human needs are insatiable; as one need is satisfied, another emerges (Maslow's principle).
  4. Complex & Individualized: Different people respond differently to identical incentives based on personal values.
  5. Dual Mechanism: Can be positive (rewards, recognition) or negative (fear of demotion, disciplinary action).

Q9. What is Management by Objectives (MBO)?

🔥 Repeated: 2016, 2017, 2020 3 Marks
✍️ Model Answer:

Management by Objectives (MBO), popularized by Peter F. Drucker in 1954 in The Practice of Management, is a strategic management model where superiors and subordinates jointly identify common goals, define each individual's major areas of responsibility in terms of expected results, and use these measures as guides for operating the unit and assessing contributions.

Core Rule: All objectives must follow the SMART criteria: Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, and Time-bound.

Q10. What are the different types of plans in an organisation?

🔥 Repeated: 2017, 2020 3 Marks
✍️ Model Answer:

Managerial plans are classified along two primary dimensions:

  1. Based on Scope & Horizon:
    • Strategic Plans: Long-term (3–5 years), top management level, enterprise-wide vision.
    • Tactical Plans: Medium-term (1–2 years), departmental level, resource allocation.
    • Operational Plans: Short-term (daily/monthly), supervisory level, specific workflows.
  2. Based on Repetitiveness of Use:
    • Standing Plans: Formulated for ongoing, recurring activities (Policies, Procedures, Rules, Methods).
    • Single-Use Plans: Developed for non-recurring, unique projects (Budgets, Programs, Schedules).

Q11. What are the social responsibilities of business (CSR)?

🔥 Repeated: 2018 3 Marks
✍️ Model Answer:

Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR) is the moral and legal obligation of business enterprises to make decisions and take actions that protect and enhance the welfare of society alongside pursuing profits:

  • Towards Shareholders / Investors: Safeguarding capital, transparent accounting, and earning fair returns.
  • Towards Employees: Fair wages, safe and ergonomic working conditions, job security, and dignity.
  • Towards Consumers: Delivering safe, quality goods at reasonable prices without deceptive advertising.
  • Towards Society & Environment: Preventing environmental pollution, ecological conservation, local employment, and educational/healthcare philanthropy.

Q12. What is an organisation chart?

🔥 Repeated: 2018 3 Marks
✍️ Model Answer:

An Organisation Chart is a diagrammatic or visual blueprint that illustrates the formal structural relationships, division of labor, hierarchy, chain of command, and reporting channels within an enterprise.

Three Standard Formats:

  1. Vertical / Top-Down Chart: Highest authority at the top; lines flow downward (most common).
  2. Horizontal / Left-to-Right Chart: Supreme executive at far left; authority flows rightward.
  3. Circular / Concentric Chart: Chief executive sits at the center circle; subordinate layers radiate outward in concentric rings.

Q13. What are the different managerial skills according to Robert Katz?

🔥 Repeated: 2018 3 Marks
✍️ Model Answer:

According to Robert L. Katz (1955), effective managerial performance requires three fundamental categories of skills:

  1. Technical Skills: Proficiency in specific methods, procedures, techniques, and specialized tools (crucial for lower/first-line managers).
  2. Human / Interpersonal Skills: The ability to communicate, motivate, lead, resolve conflict, and work effectively with and through people (equally essential across all levels).
  3. Conceptual Skills: The cognitive ability to see the organization as a whole, understand complex interrelationships, and formulate long-range strategic plans (most critical for top-level executives).

Controlling is a cybernetic closed-loop process consisting of five sequential steps:

1. Establish Performance Standards (Measurable benchmarks, KPIs, quotas)

    ↓

    ↓

Q15. What do you understand by 'Management by Objectives' (MBO)? Explain its process, benefits, and limitations.

🔥 Repeated: 2016 7.5 Marks
✍️ Model Answer:

Management by Objectives (MBO) is a results-oriented collaborative managerial philosophy pioneered by Peter Drucker. Rather than exercising top-down autocratic control, MBO aligns individual employee targets directly with overarching organizational goals through participatory goal setting.

The 5-Step MBO Process Cycle:
  1. Formulation of Organizational Goals: Top management defines corporate mission, annual growth targets, and strategic milestones.
  2. Setting Subordinate Objectives Jointly: Superior and subordinate meet collaboratively to define specific, measurable targets (SMART) for the subordinate's role.
  3. Developing Action Plans: Devising operational pathways, timelines, and resource allocations needed to accomplish the agreed targets.
  4. Periodic Performance Review: Conducting regular milestone check-ins to monitor progress and provide constructive feedback without micromanagement.
  5. Final Performance Appraisal & Reward: Evaluating annual achievement against initial targets and linking results directly to promotions, bonuses, and professional development.
Benefits & Limitations of MBO:
  • Benefits: Clarifies role expectations, increases employee commitment through participation, enhances objective performance measurement, and improves superior-subordinate trust.
  • Limitations: Highly time-consuming paperwork, excessive focus on short-term quantifiable goals at the expense of qualitative excellence, and inflexibility in rapidly shifting market conditions.

Q16. What is the role and importance of controlling in an organisation?

🔥 Repeated: 2016 7.5 Marks
✍️ Model Answer:

Controlling is the managerial function that ensures organizational activities conform to planned standards and that resources are utilized efficiently. Without control, plans remain mere wishful thinking.

Critical Roles and Organizational Benefits:
  • 1. Goal Realization: Continuously monitors actual progress against strategic milestones, flagging deviations before they cause catastrophic failure.
  • 2. Optimal Resource Utilization: Restricts waste, pilferage, redundancy, and inefficient machine idle-time through variance analysis.
  • 3. Judging Accuracy of Standards: Periodic reviews help managers verify whether established benchmarks are realistic or need upward/downward revision in response to external shifts.
  • 4. Boosting Employee Motivation: Clear, measurable standards inform workers exactly how their performance will be evaluated, reducing anxiety and favoritism.
  • 5. Ensuring Workplace Discipline: Regular surveillance, audits, and checks minimize fraud, negligence, and behavioral misconduct.
  • 6. Facilitating Organizational Coordination: Unifies the efforts of disparate functional departments (marketing, production, finance) toward common corporate targets.

Q18. Discuss the contribution of the 'Hawthorne Experiments' in the development of managerial thinking.

🔥 Repeated: 2017 7.5 Marks
✍️ Model Answer:

The Hawthorne Experiments (1924–1932), conducted by Elton Mayo and Fritz Roethlisberger at Western Electric's Hawthorne Plant in Chicago, revolutionized management theory by overturning Classical Scientific Management (Taylorism) and founding the Human Relations Movement.

The Four Experimental Phases:
  1. Illumination Experiments (1924–1927): Studied lighting levels on worker productivity. Surprisingly, output rose even when lighting was reduced to moonlight levels, proving physical variables were secondary.
  2. Relay Assembly Test Room (1927–1928): Introduced rest breaks, shorter hours, and free snacks for female assembly workers. Output climbed steadily because workers felt special, listened to, and bonded as a cohesive social unit.
  3. Mass Interviewing Program (1928–1930): Interviewed 21,000+ employees, discovering that worker productivity was heavily governed by personal emotional attitudes and social standing within peer groups.
  4. Bank Wiring Observation Room (1931–1932): Observed a male group that enforced an unwritten informal production ceiling ("fair day's work") to protect slower peers, punishing overproducers as "rate-busters".
Core Contributions to Modern Management:
  • The 'Hawthorne Effect': Employees work harder and perform better simply when they feel managers observe and genuinely care about their wellbeing.
  • Workplace is a Social System: Workers are not merely rational economic beings ("economic men"), but complex social individuals driven by social recognition, security, and belonging.
  • Informal Groups: Informal social networks and peer norms inside an enterprise often exert greater influence over output than formal managerial rules.

Q19. Write a short note on Span of Control. Compare Narrow Span vs Wide Span.

🔥 Repeated: 2017 7.5 Marks
✍️ Model Answer:

Span of Control (also known as Span of Management) refers to the total number of subordinates that a single manager can supervise directly, efficiently, and effectively. French management consultant V.A. Graicunas demonstrated that as subordinates increase linearly ($n$), the complex interpersonal relationships multiply geometrically according to $R = n(2^{n-1} + n - 1)$.

Q20. What are the internal and external factors affecting the decision-making process?

🔥 Repeated: 2017 7.5 Marks
✍️ Model Answer:

Decision-making is the conscious cognitive process of selecting the most optimal course of action from among available alternatives to achieve organizational objectives. It is constrained by complex multidimensional factors:

1. Internal Organizational Factors:
  • Organizational Culture & Values: Risk-tolerant cultures (e.g., tech startups) foster bold, rapid innovation; bureaucratic firms favor slow, precedent-based risk avoidance.
  • Resource Availability: Financial capital, technical machinery, and skilled human personnel limit the feasible options.
  • Decision-Maker's Psychology & Cognitive Biases: Personal values, risk appetite, confirmation bias, intuition, and experience.
  • Information Quality & Time Pressures: Severe time pressure often forces managers to rely on Herbert Simon's Bounded Rationality (satisficing rather than perfect optimizing).
2. External Environmental Factors:
  • Market & Competitive Dynamics: Competitors' retaliatory pricing, substitute products, and supplier bargaining power.
  • Legal & Regulatory Environment: Labor laws, trade tariffs, anti-monopoly statutes, and environmental compliance rules.
  • Technological Uncertainty: Rapid technological disruption renders decisions obsolete if implementation takes too long.

Q21. Define Strategy. Explain its meaning and three hierarchical levels.

🔥 Repeated: 2018 7.5 Marks
✍️ Model Answer:

Strategy is a comprehensive, unified, and integrated master plan formulated by top leadership that links the strategic advantages of the firm to the challenges of the external environment to ensure organizational survival and competitive dominance.

Alfred Chandler defined strategy as: "The determination of the basic long-term goals and objectives of an enterprise, and the adoption of courses of action and the allocation of resources necessary for carrying out these goals."

The Three Hierarchical Levels of Strategy:
  1. Corporate-Level Strategy: Formulated by the Board of Directors and CEO. Answers: "What businesses should we be in?" Encompasses decisions regarding corporate diversification, mergers, acquisitions, and resource allocation across business divisions (e.g., Tata Group managing Steel, Motors, and TCS).
  2. Business-Unit Level Strategy (SBU): Formulated by Strategic Business Unit leaders. Answers: "How do we compete successfully in our specific industry/market?" Focuses on Michael Porter's Cost Leadership, Differentiation, or Focus niche.
  3. Functional-Level Strategy: Formulated by departmental heads (Marketing, Finance, R&D, HR, Operations). Answers: "How can departmental operations support the business strategy?" E.g., digital marketing campaigns, just-in-time inventory.

Q22. Define stress. Explain the concept of stress management and workplace coping strategies.

🔥 Repeated: 2018, 2020 7.5 Marks
✍️ Model Answer:

Stress is an adaptive psychological and physiological response to external situational demands (stressors) that place excessive pressure on an individual's coping capacity. Mild stress that stimulates peak performance is termed Eustress (positive stress), whereas excessive, chronic stress leading to exhaustion and burnout is termed Distress.

Primary Workplace Stressors:
  • Role Ambiguity & Role Conflict: Unclear job duties or conflicting demands from multiple superiors.
  • Work Overload & Tight Deadlines: Unrealistic targets causing prolonged overtime and sleep deprivation.
  • Lack of Autonomy & Job Insecurity: Micromanagement combined with constant threats of layoffs.
  • Interpersonal Friction: Toxic workplace politics, bullying, or lack of social support.
Stress Management Coping Techniques:

Q23. Distinguish between Authority and Responsibility. Explain the Principle of Parity.

🔥 Repeated: 2018, 2020 7.5 Marks
✍️ Model Answer:

Authority is the legitimate right of a manager to make decisions, give orders, and command obedience from subordinates. Responsibility is the moral and contractual obligation of a subordinate to execute assigned duties faithfully and effectively.

Comparison ParameterAuthorityResponsibility
NatureLegitimate institutional power to command.Moral/legal obligation to perform assigned work.

DelegationCan be delegated systematically to others.Cannot be completely abdicated; primary responsibility remains.

EnforcementBacked by disciplinary rewards and penalties.Governed by personal commitment, duty, and ethics.
Fayol's Principle of Parity of Authority and Responsibility:

Henri Fayol emphasized that Authority and Responsibility must be equal and co-extensive:

  • If Authority > Responsibility: The manager possesses arbitrary power without accountability, leading to misuse of power and managerial tyranny.
  • If Responsibility > Authority: The subordinate is held accountable for results without the necessary power to command resources, causing extreme frustration, helplessness, and operational paralysis.

Q24. Explain the concept of Management of Change. Why is change inevitable in modern business?

🔥 Repeated: 2018 7.5 Marks
✍️ Model Answer:

Management of Change is the structured, systematic approach to transitioning individuals, teams, and whole organizations from a current state to a desired future state to achieve competitive advantage and survive environmental disruption.

Why Change is Inevitable in Modern Business:
  • Technological Hyper-Evolution: Companies that fail to adopt modern IT, automation, AI, and digital transformation risk extinction (e.g., Kodak, Blockbuster).
  • Globalized Market Competition: Lower trade barriers bring aggressive international competitors into local domestic markets.
  • Shifting Workforce Demographics: The influx of digitally native generations (Gen Z) demands flatter structures, flexible work, and purpose-driven corporate cultures.
  • Economic & Geopolitical Shocks: Supply chain fractures, sudden inflationary spikes, and regulatory shifts require rapid strategic pivots.

Proactive vs. Reactive Change: Proactive change anticipates future disruptions and initiates reforms ahead of rivals; Reactive change is forced upon a reluctant enterprise by an external crisis.

Q25. Critically analyse Abraham Maslow's 'Hierarchy of Needs' theory of motivation. Explain its five levels, merits, limitations, and relevance to modern managers.

🔥 Repeated: 2016 15 Marks
✍️ Model Answer:

In 1943, American psychologist Abraham H. Maslow published his groundbreaking paper "A Theory of Human Motivation", proposing that human behavior is driven by an underlying hierarchy of five innate basic need categories arranged in order of prepotency.

🔺 FIGURE 1 : Maslow's Need Hierarchy Pyramid (CCSU Dec 2016) Authentic Vector SVG Diagram
Comprehensive Breakdown of the 5 Levels:
  1. Physiological Needs: Basic biological necessities for human survival (air, water, food, sleep). In organizational terms: basic salary, clean air, drinking water, and lunch breaks.
  2. Safety and Security Needs: Freedom from physical danger, financial insecurity, and emotional threat. Organizationally: job tenure security, health insurance, provident fund, and safe factory conditions.
  3. Social (Belongingness) Needs: The human desire for affectionate relationships, acceptance, and social identity. Organizationally: cohesive work teams, pleasant interpersonal culture, and company recreational events.
  4. Esteem Needs: Self-respect, competence, independence (internal esteem) and recognition, status, attention, and appreciation from others (external esteem). Organizationally: job titles, merit awards, promotion pathways, and executive perks.
  5. Self-Actualisation Needs: The pinnacle desire to realize one's full personal potential and "become everything one is capable of becoming." Organizationally: challenging assignments, freedom for innovation, and creative autonomy.
Critical Appraisal & Limitations:
  • Lack of Strict Empirical Hierarchy: Real-world human needs do not always follow a rigid lock-step sequence. For instance, an impoverished artist may pursue self-actualization despite lacking physiological security.
  • Simultaneous Multi-Need Activation: In modern knowledge workplaces, employees are frequently motivated by social, esteem, and financial needs simultaneously.
  • Cultural Bias: Maslow's model reflects Western individualistic values; in collectivist cultures (e.g., India, Japan), social and family acceptance often supersedes individual self-esteem.

Q26. What is the need and importance of delegation of authority? What difficulties and barriers arise in delegation, and how can they be overcome?

🔥 Repeated: 2016 15 Marks
✍️ Model Answer:

Delegation of Authority is the organizational process whereby a manager transfers a portion of their official authority and operational duties to immediate subordinates, while retaining ultimate accountability for the final results.

The Three Inseparable Elements of Delegation:
  1. Assignment of Responsibility: Entrusting specific tasks, duties, and operational deliverables to a subordinate.
  2. Granting of Authority: Conferring necessary legal rights, budget approvals, and command power to execute the duties.
  3. Creation of Accountability: The subordinate's absolute obligation to report performance outcomes back to the delegator. Authority can be delegated, but ultimate accountability can never be shifted!
Need and Importance of Delegation:
  • Relieves Top Management Overload: Frees senior leaders from routine operational firefighting, enabling them to concentrate on long-range strategic planning.
  • Accelerates Decision Making: Operational problems are resolved swiftly at the spot without awaiting multi-tiered bureaucratic clearances.
  • Employee Development & Succession Planning: Hands-on operational responsibility trains promising subordinates for future managerial promotion.
  • Enhances Workplace Morale: Demonstrates executive trust, instilling pride, ownership, and commitment in subordinates.
Difficulties & Barriers in Delegation:
Obstacles on the Part of SuperiorsObstacles on the Part of Subordinates
"I can do it better myself" fallacy: Egotistical belief that subordinates lack the skill to match the manager's perfectionism.Fear of Failure & Criticism: Hesitation to accept new authority due to anxiety over punitive reprimands for honest mistakes.
Fear of Insecurity / Replacement: Anxiety that a highly capable subordinate might outshine and displace the superior.Lack of Confidence & Training: Inadequate technical preparation or knowledge needed to execute the duties.
Lack of Trust in Subordinates: Micromanagement mindset unwilling to let others take autonomous initiative.Absence of Commensurate Incentives: Reluctance to take on extra burden without financial rewards or recognition.
Guidelines for Effective Delegation:
  • Parity Principle: Ensure granted authority is strictly commensurate with assigned responsibility.
  • Clear Goal Definition: Define expected performance deliverables unambiguously.
  • Supportive Feedback & Psychological Safety: Coach subordinates through early errors rather than punishing honest initiatives.

Q27. What do you understand by the term 'Strategy'? How are modern companies changing their strategies to compete in the new business environment?

🔥 Repeated: 2016, 2020 15 Marks
✍️ Model Answer:

A Strategy is a unified, comprehensive, and action-oriented master blueprint designed to position an enterprise advantageously against competitors and navigate turbulent external market forces to achieve sustainable, long-term corporate goals.

Forces Reshaping the Modern Business Environment:
  • Digital Disruption: Proliferation of Cloud computing, Generative AI, Big Data analytics, and ubiquitous mobile connectivity.
  • Globalized Volatility (VUCA): Volatile, Uncertain, Complex, and Ambiguous international markets with fragile cross-border supply chains.
  • Shifting Customer Power: Omnichannel consumer access demanding instant gratification, ethical sustainability, and hyper-personalized products.
How Leading Companies are Changing Strategic Approaches:
  1. From Asset-Heavy to Platform & Ecosystem Strategies: Traditional firms invested in heavy physical assets (factories, retail stores). Modern leaders leverage digital multi-sided platforms (e.g., Uber owns no cars, Airbnb owns no real estate, Apple App Store captures high margins).
  2. Adopting Agile & Adaptive Strategic Execution: Abandoning rigid 5-year plans in favor of continuous iterative strategy sprints with rapid testing, pivoting, and continuous deployment.
  3. Customer Experience & Value-Chain Digitalization: Integrating IoT, CRM, and automated logistics to provide seamless omnichannel customer support (e.g., Amazon Prime, Domino's digital pizza tracker).
  4. Sustainability and ESG (Environmental, Social, Governance): Incorporating carbon-neutral operations, ethical sourcing, and renewable energy as core marketing differentiators rather than afterthought compliance.
  5. Strategic Alliances & Co-opetition: Competing fiercely in core areas while partnering in foundational technology research (e.g., Sony and Microsoft partnering on cloud gaming infrastructure).

Q28. Explain the need and importance of coordination in an organisation. Why is coordination termed the 'Essence of Management'?

🔥 Repeated: 2016 15 Marks
✍️ Model Answer:

Coordination is the orderly synchronization and integration of group efforts to provide unity of action in the pursuit of common enterprise goals. Eminent theorists Koontz and O'Donnell noted: "Coordination is not a separate function of management, but the very essence of managership."

Why Coordination is the 'Essence of Management':

Coordination permeates every single managerial function like a unifying golden thread:

  • In Planning: Coordinates the individual departmental plans (Production, Marketing, HR, Finance) with the master corporate blueprint.
  • In Organizing: Coordinates diverse specialized roles, authority levels, and resources to prevent redundant bottlenecks.
  • In Staffing: Aligns individual worker competencies with specific job requirements.
  • In Directing: Harmonizes leadership, motivation, and communication so team members pull in the same direction.
  • In Controlling: Harmonizes ongoing performance with established standards across all units.
Mary Parker Follett's Four Classic Principles of Coordination:
  1. Principle of Direct Contact: Coordination is most effective when managers communicate face-to-face horizontally across departments rather than strictly through formal vertical chains.
  2. Principle of Early Stage: Coordination must begin at the earliest stages of planning and policy formulation, before rigid departmental silos solidify.
  3. Principle of Reciprocal Relationship: All factors in a situation are interrelated (e.g., a change in sales forecasts instantly impacts inventory, cash flow, and staffing).
  4. Principle of Continuous Process: Coordination is not a one-off event; it is an ongoing, continuous operational necessity.

Q29. How can change be successfully managed in an organisation? Explain Kurt Lewin's 3-Step Change Model and strategies to overcome resistance to change.

🔥 Repeated: 2016, 2018 15 Marks
✍️ Model Answer:

Managing organizational change requires overcoming psychological inertia and social resistance. Social psychologist Kurt Lewin (1947) formulated the foundational Three-Step Model of Change based on Force-Field Analysis, which posits that any current operational state exists in equilibrium between Driving Forces (pushing for change) and Restraining Forces (resisting change).

🔄 FIGURE 2 : Kurt Lewin's 3-Step Change Process (CCSU Dec 2016) Authentic Vector SVG Diagram
The Three Phases Explained:
  1. Unfreezing: Dismantling current beliefs and operational habits. Managers must create psychological safety, communicate the compelling necessity of change, and weaken restraining forces.
  2. Changing (Movement): The actual implementation phase. Employees learn new software, workflows, and reporting structures through rigorous coaching, mentoring, and continuous support.
  3. Refreezing: Solidifying the new behaviors into company culture to prevent regression to old habits. Formalized through new standard operating procedures (SOPs), KPI evaluations, and reward systems.
John Kotter's Strategies to Overcome Resistance to Change:
  • Education & Transparent Communication: Demystifying the change through workshops to eliminate rumors and uncertainty.
  • Participation & Involvement: Involving resistant employees directly in the design of the new system to give them psychological ownership.
  • Facilitation & Emotional Support: Providing stress counseling and patient training for those struggling with new digital tools.
  • Incentives & Negotiation: Offering early bonuses, promotions, or retraining packages to key stakeholders.

Q30. Define ethics and social responsibility of business. What are the key tools of ethics, and what are an enterprise's responsibilities toward different stakeholders?

🔥 Repeated: 2017, 2020 15 Marks
✍️ Model Answer:

Business Ethics refers to the system of moral principles, rules, and values that guide managerial behavior, decisions, and commercial practices regarding what is right and wrong in business conduct. Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR) is the continuing commitment by business to behave ethically and contribute to economic development while improving the quality of life of the workforce, local community, and society at large.

Tools of Business Ethics:
  1. Code of Conduct (Ethical Charter): A formally published legal and moral handbook outlining mandatory conduct regarding bribery, conflicts of interest, discrimination, and data privacy.
  2. Ethics Committees & Officers: An independent board committee charged with overseeing ethical compliance and investigating misconduct.
  3. Ethics Training Programs: Interactive seminars and role-playing workshops to help managers handle grey-area moral dilemmas.
  4. Whistleblower Hotlines & Protection: Confidential, anonymous reporting channels where employees can expose financial fraud or safety violations without fear of retaliation.
  5. Social Audits: Independent evaluations of an enterprise's social, environmental, and labor footprint.
Archie Carroll's 4-Part CSR Pyramid & Stakeholder Duties:
  • Economic Responsibility (Be Profitable): The foundation; producing goods efficiently and generating fair returns for shareholders.
  • Legal Responsibility (Obey the Law): Complying rigorously with taxation, labor safety, environmental, and anti-monopoly statutes.
  • Ethical Responsibility (Be Ethical): Doing what is right, just, and fair, even when not codified by statute (e.g., honest advertising, paying living wages).
  • Philanthropic Responsibility (Be a Good Citizen): Voluntary corporate contributions to community healthcare, education, disaster relief, and green reforestation.

Q31. "Planning without control is meaningless and control without planning is a waste." Critically discuss the close, complementary relationship between planning and controlling.

🔥 Repeated: 2017, 2020 15 Marks
✍️ Model Answer:

Planning and controlling are universally acknowledged as the "Siamese Twins of Management". They represent two inseparable sides of the same managerial coin; neither can function effectively without the other.

🔄 FIGURE 3 : Cybernetic Closed-Loop of Planning and Control (CCSU Dec 2017 & 2020) Authentic Vector SVG Diagram
Critical Analysis of the Two Propositons:
  1. "Planning without control is meaningless":
    • Planning merely determines what should be done in the future. It creates paper goals, schedules, and budgets.
    • If there is no controlling system to measure actual progress, workers can ignore plans with impunity. Deviations will accumulate unchecked, causing waste and inevitable failure. Thus, planning remains meaningless without control to enforce execution.
  2. "Control without planning is a waste / blind":
    • Controlling involves comparing actual performance against predefined benchmarks.
    • If no prior planning exists, there are no objectives, quotas, budgets, or quality metrics. A manager cannot judge whether production of 1,000 units is good or bad without a planned target. Exercising control in the dark is arbitrary, futile, and a total waste of managerial energy.

Conclusion: Planning is forward-looking (anticipating future paths), whereas controlling is backward-looking (evaluating past results). Through continuous feedback loops, controlling informs future planning, sustaining corporate survival.

Q34. Explain the Systems Approach of modern management theory. Describe an organisation as an Open Dynamic System with inputs, transformation, outputs, and feedback loops.

🔥 Repeated: 2018, 2020 15 Marks
✍️ Model Answer:

Developed during the 1960s (pioneered by Ludwig von Bertalanffy, Chester Barnard, and Kenneth Boulding), the Systems Approach views an organization not as a collection of isolated functional silos, but as a complex, unified, purposeful system of interrelated and interdependent subsystems operating within a dynamic external environment.

🌐 FIGURE 5 : Open Systems Architecture of Management (CCSU Dec 2018 & 2020) Authentic Vector SVG Diagram
Salient Concepts of the Systems Approach:
  • Open System vs. Closed System: Closed systems ignore external stimuli; modern enterprises are strictly Open Systems that import energy, materials, and information from the environment and export finished outputs back into it.
  • Synergy ($2 + 2 = 5$ Effect): The integrated whole organization produces greater output and value than the sum of its individual departments operating independently.
  • Subsystem Interdependence: A disruption in one subsystem (e.g., strikes in supply logistics) directly impairs production, marketing deliveries, and corporate cash flow.
  • Entropy vs. Negative Entropy: Natural systems tend toward decay and disorder (entropy). Organizations stave off extinction by importing resources and innovating (negative entropy).
  • Equifinality: Management can achieve the same ultimate objective using diverse alternative pathways, methods, and inputs.

Q35. Describe the concepts and core pillars of Total Quality Management (TQM). Explain Deming's PDCA Cycle and discuss emerging challenges in modern quality management.

🔥 Repeated: 2018 15 Marks
✍️ Model Answer:

Total Quality Management (TQM) is an enterprise-wide management philosophy formulated by quality pioneers like W. Edwards Deming, Joseph M. Juran, and Philip Crosby. It aims for long-term corporate success through total customer satisfaction, requiring all organizational members to participate in continuous process improvement.

🔄 FIGURE 6 : Deming's Continuous Improvement PDCA Cycle (CCSU Dec 2018) Authentic Vector SVG Diagram
The Three Pillars of TQM:
  1. Customer Satisfaction: Quality is judged ultimately by the user. Zero defects and high reliability protect brand loyalty.
  2. Continuous Process Improvement (Kaizen): Incremental, unending upgrades to workflows rather than waiting for catastrophic breakdowns.
  3. Total Employee Involvement: Empowering frontline operators through Quality Circles to detect and solve defects immediately.
Emerging Issues & Challenges in Modern Quality Management:
  • Cultural Resistance: Middle managers may resist shifting from bureaucratic inspection to participatory employee empowerment.
  • Short-Term Profit Pressures: Wall Street quarterly profit expectations often undermine long-term capital investments in TQM training.
  • Global Supply Chain Fragmentation: Enforcing strict Six Sigma quality tolerances across outsourced foreign third-party vendors is notoriously difficult.
  • Software & Digital Quality: Transitioning TQM principles from tangible physical manufacturing to intangible digital software and cloud service reliability.
